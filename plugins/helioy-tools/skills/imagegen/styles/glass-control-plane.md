# Glass Control Plane

Use this style for visuals that should make an abstract system feel like a
physical operating surface: transparent glass, machined modules, routed
capabilities, and one live signal path. It is built from the Helix control board
direction, but reusable for other contexts where the argument is:

```text
central engine -> connected capabilities -> live operational state
```

## Core Effect

Make the viewer feel that the system is a premium instrument panel for thought,
context, orchestration, or infrastructure.

The image should feel cinematic, tangible, precise, and controlled. It should
not feel like a flat dashboard, a generic network graph, a neon reactor, or a
busy enterprise architecture diagram.

## Visual Grammar

- Wide cinematic 16:9 hero composition.
- Large transparent glass or acrylic plane in three quarter perspective.
- Rounded beveled outer frame with bright rim highlights.
- Dark etched grid inside the glass, visible only as subtle structure.
- Black graphite module cards with glossy bevels and white edge glints.
- Central engine block larger than the surrounding modules.
- Surrounding capability modules arranged left and right of the center.
- Physical pipe conduits, collars, elbows, clamps, and coupling rings.
- One restrained green live signal spine, usually vertical.
- Small white etched icons and sparse label plates.
- Low black environment with controlled smoke, fiber mist, or vapor at the base.
- White, graphite, chrome, and restrained signal green as the main palette.

## Composition Pattern

Glass plane:

- The plane sits slightly tilted in depth, like a high end transparent control
  board photographed in a dark studio.
- The outer frame must read as physical glass with thickness, refraction, and
  reflected highlights.
- Use a subtle internal grid to establish scale and engineering discipline.

Center:

- Put the primary engine, concept, product, or system in the center.
- Use a square or rounded square graphite enclosure with machined corners.
- Add a Helix inspired aperture, split circle, lens, or hub mark when useful.
- The center should feel locked into the board through clamps and conduits.

Left and right modules:

- Use three to eight capability modules around the center.
- Each module is a black glass or graphite card with bevels and metal side caps.
- Connect each module to the center with short physical pipework.
- Modules may contain simple white line icons, one short title, and one short
  subtitle when text precision is not critical.

Live signal:

- Use one green signal path as the state indicator.
- The signal can pass through the center and include small glowing nodes.
- Keep glow restrained, crisp, and vertical or orthogonal.
- Do not turn the signal into lightning, plasma, a laser grid, or a reactor beam.

Footer rail:

- Optional bottom row of compact legend icons and status marks.
- Treat this as etched instrumentation, not a full interface.
- Keep it low contrast and secondary to the central system.

## Text Handling

This style often wants labels, but generated text is fragile.

For production work:

- Generate blank module plates or intentionally minimal placeholder labels.
- Add final titles, subtitles, and legend text deterministically afterward.
- If direct generation of text is acceptable, use very short labels only.
- Prefer all caps mono for section labels and clean sans for module names.

Good label scale:

- Section labels: two to four words.
- Module titles: one to two words.
- Subtitles: two to four words.

Avoid dense paragraphs, tiny annotations, long product claims, and many labels
with similar lengths.

## Materials

Use:

- smoked glass
- black acrylic
- dark graphite
- chrome bevels
- polished steel collars
- rubber gasket seams
- translucent green light nodes
- faint etched grid lines

The image should have photographic material density. Glass, metal, and smoke
must feel physically lit.

## Lighting

Default mood:

- Near black studio environment.
- Strong white rim highlights on glass edges and module bevels.
- Small green glow only around the signal spine and state nodes.
- Subtle top or side reflections across the transparent board.
- Low vapor at the bottom edge for depth and scale.

Avoid daylight, colorful gradients, strong purple, cyberpunk city lighting,
heavy bloom, and all over particle effects.

## Reuse Guidance

Adapt the subject by changing the central engine and surrounding module set.
Preserve the physical control plane, module cards, conduits, and restrained
green signal spine.

Examples:

- A writing engine connected to research, memory, critique, voice, and publish
  modules.
- A product operating system connected to customers, billing, support, telemetry,
  and roadmap modules.
- A context engine connected to retrieval, decisions, code, attention, agents,
  and transport modules.
- A security system connected to identity, policy, audit, detection, response,
  and recovery modules.

When the context changes, keep the style adult, sparse, mechanical, and precise.

# Iceberg Operating Plane

Use this style for visuals that should express a simple visible surface action
resting on a large, structured, hidden system. It is built from the Helioy
iceberg hero direction, but reusable for other contexts where the argument is:

```text
small surface signal -> deep hidden structure -> locked operating plane
```

## Core Effect

Make the viewer feel that the visible action is only the accessible tip of a
much larger context system.

The image should feel cinematic, physical, and controlled. It should not feel
like generic sci fi, a neon reactor, a dashboard, or a fantasy environment.

## Visual Grammar

- Wide cinematic 16:9 hero composition.
- Main subject weighted to the right.
- Broad clean copy safe negative space on the left.
- Split waterline or equivalent threshold between visible surface and hidden
  depth.
- Small visible surface portion, about 15 to 20 percent of total subject height.
- Large underwater or hidden mass, about 80 to 85 percent of total subject
  height.
- Dark arctic or low key environment with a modest brightness lift for detail.
- Photographic material density, not flat illustration.
- Matte black or graphite mechanical connector.
- Connector visible above the surface, hidden through the middle mass, then
  reemerging only at the operating plane connection.
- Helix inspired center module: graphite aperture, convex glass, split circle
  motif, pipe like couplings, small status points, and motherboard like panels.

## Composition Pattern

Left side:

- Empty dark water, sky, atmosphere, or smooth environmental gradient.
- Reserved for future HTML text, logo, or headline.
- No generated copy, labels, slogans, or UI.

Right side:

- The surface subject sits above the threshold.
- The full hidden mass remains visible below the threshold.
- The subject must read as one coherent physical object.
- The silhouette should be clear before internal detail appears.

Threshold:

- Use a crisp horizontal waterline, surface plane, ice shelf, glass layer, or
  other clear boundary.
- The threshold must feel physically real, with reflection, refraction, and
  small surface distortion.

Depth:

- The hidden mass should have layered structure, refraction, bubbles, scratches,
  chips, sediment like internal shadows, and natural falloff.
- Complexity should be organized, not noisy.

## Connector Rule

The connector is a hero element, but it should not become a continuous beam.

Required behavior:

```text
visible top connector -> hidden inside mass -> short reemergent connector at center module
```

Details:

- Use matte black graphite or black anodized metal.
- Make the top connector thick enough to read at hero scale.
- Add subtle glossy edge highlights and a small split circle mark when useful.
- Hide the middle section behind or inside the main mass.
- Reemerge only at the physical connection to the center module.
- Do not run the connector through the glass center.
- Do not render the connector as light, lightning, laser, or a thin line.

## Center Module

The center module is the operating plane. It should feel mechanically locked
into the hidden structure.

Use:

- dark graphite aperture assembly
- thick convex glass pressure window
- split circular Helix motif
- machined retaining rings
- compression clamps
- pressure seals
- pipe like perimeter couplings
- motherboard like panels and traces behind glass
- small restrained green, amber, or cyan status points
- optical distortion, condensation, scratches, and reflections on glass

Avoid:

- humans
- chairs
- desks
- furniture
- readable UI text
- pasted flat screens
- literal logo panels unless explicitly requested
- continuous green vertical line through the full mass

## Lighting

Default mood:

- Dark polar blue, moonlit, low key.
- Slightly lifted shadows so ice and hardware remain readable.
- Restrained contrast and restrained bloom.
- Subtle aurora or sky glow only when asked.

Do not turn the style into daylight unless the user explicitly asks.

## Environment Discipline

Use open environment and depth falloff.

Avoid:

- mountains
- seabed
- rocks
- terrain ridges
- distant land
- cities
- labs
- busy sci fi backgrounds
- storm effects
- lightning
- neon beams
- cartoon energy
- drill bits
- watermarks

## Prompt Template

```text
Create a [format] in the iceberg-operating-plane style.

Subject: [surface subject and hidden system].

Core metaphor:
small surface signal -> deep hidden structure -> locked operating plane.

Composition:
- Wide cinematic 16:9 hero.
- Left: [percentage] clean copy safe dark negative space.
- Right: [main subject] with a small visible surface portion and large hidden
  underwater or lower mass.
- Threshold: crisp horizontal [waterline, surface plane, glass layer, or other
  boundary] with realistic reflection and refraction.

Proportion:
- Visible surface portion is 15 to 20 percent of total subject height.
- Hidden mass is 80 to 85 percent of total subject height.

Connector:
- Matte black graphite connector visible above the threshold.
- Connector disappears inside the mass through the middle.

Center module:
- Helix inspired graphite aperture assembly.
- Thick convex glass pressure window.

Materials:
- dense ice, glass, dark graphite, black anodized metal

Lighting:
- dark polar blue, moonlit, readable low key

Avoid:
- generated text, labels, watermark
- humans, furniture, dashboard UI
- mountains, seabed, terrain, distant land
- lightning, neon beam, energy storm, drill bit
```

## Reuse Guidance

The style can be adapted beyond literal icebergs. Preserve the structure:

```text
visible tip + clean threshold + hidden mass + mechanical connector + operating plane
```

Examples:

- A small public post above water, with deep research infrastructure below.
- A simple command surface, with a large hidden orchestration engine below.
- A product button above a glass plane, with context machinery underneath.
- A clean API call above the threshold, with memory and retrieval systems below.

When changing the subject, keep the composition, proportion, connector behavior,
and center module logic intact.
